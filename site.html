IThe House

Adda is Bengali for the conversation that keeps you at the table long after the plates are cleared.

On Frankford Avenue, across from the Fishtown post office, Chef Chintan Pandya and Roni Mazumdar bring the cooking of India’s homes, highway dhabas and wedding kitchens to Philadelphia.

Region by region, spice by spice. An open kitchen and grill on the ground floor, a small bar below, and a dining room built for staying longer than you planned.

IIIThe Menu

A menu of places

Every plate comes from somewhere specific: a Mumbai street, a Hyderabad wedding, a Rajput hunting camp. We cook it the way it is cooked there.

Illustrative menu for this presentation, drawn from Adda’s best-known dishes. Menus change with the season.

IVThe Hearth

Three fires, one kitchen

तंदूर

Tandoor

The clay oven of the north. Marinated meat and fish on iron skewers, bread slapped against the wall, charred at heat a kitchen range can’t reach.

तवा

Tawa

A flat iron over open flame. Liver seared in seconds, breads blistered by hand: the fast, loud cooking of Old Delhi’s lanes.

दम

Dum

A heavy pot sealed with dough and left on low coals. Rice and goat steam in each other’s juices until the seal is broken at your table.

VDownstairs

A room below the room

A small bar and a chef’s counter beneath the dining room, for whatever the kitchen can’t stop thinking about.

One-night menus, visiting cooks, the regional dishes that don’t fit anywhere else, and the drinks that go with them. It changes often and seats very few.
